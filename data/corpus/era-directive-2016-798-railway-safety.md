> Condensed reference summary of Directive (EU) 2016/798 (the recast Railway
> Safety Directive, part of the Fourth Railway Package) for retrieval
> purposes. Not legal text — verify against EUR-Lex.

## Articles 1–4 Scope and development of safety

The Directive establishes a common framework for railway safety in the EU:
common safety targets (CSTs), common safety methods (CSMs), common safety
indicators (CSIs), and harmonised safety certification. Member States and
the European Union Agency for Railways (ERA) must ensure railway safety is
generally maintained and continuously improved. Responsibility for safe
operation lies with the railway undertakings (RUs) and infrastructure
managers (IMs), each for its own part of the system; manufacturers,
maintainers, wagon keepers and service providers must ensure their products
and services meet safety requirements.

## Article 9 Safety management systems (SMS)

Every railway undertaking and infrastructure manager must establish a safety
management system that ensures control of all risks created by its
activities, including maintenance, material supply, and the use of
contractors. The SMS must take into account CSTs, TSIs, national rules, and
risks arising from third parties, and must ensure application of the CSM on
risk evaluation and assessment. SMS elements include: a safety policy
approved by the chief executive, qualitative and quantitative safety
targets, procedures to meet technical and operational standards, risk
assessment procedures, competence management, information sharing,
documentation, occurrence reporting and investigation, emergency plans, and
internal auditing. Annual safety reports must be submitted to the national
safety authority (NSA) by 31 May.

## Article 10 Single safety certificate

Railway undertakings need a single safety certificate to access
infrastructure. The certificate is issued by ERA (acting as one-stop shop)
where the area of operation covers more than one Member State, or, at the
applicant's choice, by the NSA where operation is limited to one Member
State. The certificate confirms the RU has established an SMS and can
operate safely in the intended area of operation. Validity is up to five
years, renewable; it must be updated in whole or in part whenever the type
or extent of operation substantially changes.

## Article 12 Safety authorisation of infrastructure managers

Infrastructure managers require a safety authorisation from the NSA of their
Member State, confirming the SMS and covering procedures to meet
requirements for safe design, maintenance and operation of the
infrastructure, including traffic management and signalling. Validity up to
five years.

## Articles 14 and 15 Maintenance of vehicles — entity in charge of maintenance

Each vehicle must have an entity in charge of maintenance (ECM) assigned and
registered in the vehicle register. The ECM ensures, by means of a system of
maintenance, that vehicles are in a safe state of running. ECMs for freight
wagons must be certified under the ECM certification scheme (now Regulation
(EU) 2019/779, extending certification to entities maintaining other
vehicle types).

## Articles 16–19 National safety authorities

Each Member State must establish an NSA, independent in organisation and
decision-making from any RU, IM, applicant or contracting entity. NSA tasks
include issuing single safety certificates (national scope), safety
authorisations, supervision of SMS compliance, and monitoring national
safety rules. NSAs must supervise RUs and IMs and may apply enforcement
measures.

## Articles 20–26 Accident and incident investigation

Member States must establish an independent national investigating body
(NIB) that investigates serious accidents (derailments/collisions with at
least one fatality or five serious injuries or extensive damage of at least
EUR 2 million) and may investigate other occurrences. Investigations are
conducted independently of any judicial inquiry, aim at safety improvement
rather than apportioning blame or liability, and result in reports with
safety recommendations addressed to NSAs or other bodies, which must be
taken into account and acted upon.
