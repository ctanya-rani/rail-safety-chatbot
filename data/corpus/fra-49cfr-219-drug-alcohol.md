## § 219.101–219.103 Prohibitions

Regulated employees may not use or possess alcohol or any controlled
substance while on duty, may not report for covered service under the
influence of or impaired by alcohol, and may not have a blood alcohol
concentration of 0.04 or greater while on duty. Use within four hours of
reporting for covered service is prohibited. Prescription and
over-the-counter medication use is permitted only consistent with medical
guidance that it does not adversely affect safe performance.

## § 219.201–219.211 Post-accident toxicological testing

Post-accident testing (blood and urine, sent to the FRA-designated
laboratory) is mandatory after qualifying events: major train accidents
(fatality, release of hazardous material with evacuation or injury, or
damage ≥ the reporting threshold in a collision/derailment), impact
accidents, fatal train incidents, and passenger train accidents with injury.
The railroad must make best efforts to obtain specimens as soon as possible
and document any failure to test.

## § 219.301–219.303 Reasonable suspicion and reasonable cause testing

A railroad must require testing when a trained supervisor has reasonable
suspicion, based on specific, contemporaneous, articulable observations,
that an employee is under the influence. Reasonable cause testing is
authorized after certain rule violations or accidents/incidents.

## § 219.601–219.605 Random testing

Each railroad must implement an FRA-approved random testing program covering
regulated employees, at minimum annual random testing rates set by FRA
(adjusted based on industry positive rates). Selections must be made by a
scientifically valid random method, and tests must be unannounced and spread
reasonably through the year.

## § 219.1001 Peer support and co-worker report policies

Railroads must adopt voluntary referral and co-worker report policies that
allow employees with substance abuse problems to self-refer for treatment
with protection from discipline, subject to conditions.
