## § 213.1–213.5 Scope and responsibility

Part 213 prescribes minimum safety requirements for railroad track that is
part of the general railroad system of transportation. The track owner is
responsible for compliance. An owner may assign responsibility for compliance
to another person (such as a lessee) by filing a written designation with FRA.
If a track segment does not meet the requirements for its intended class, the
owner must bring it into compliance, halt operations, or operate it under the
requirements of a lower class that it does meet.

## § 213.9 Classes of track and maximum allowable operating speeds

Track is designated Class 1 through Class 5 under Subpart A–F (Class 6 and
above are covered by Subpart G for high-speed operations). Maximum allowable
operating speeds:

- Class 1 track: 10 mph freight, 15 mph passenger
- Class 2 track: 25 mph freight, 30 mph passenger
- Class 3 track: 40 mph freight, 60 mph passenger
- Class 4 track: 60 mph freight, 80 mph passenger
- Class 5 track: 80 mph freight, 90 mph passenger

Excepted track (§ 213.4) may be operated at no more than 10 mph, with no
passenger trains, and no more than five cars containing hazardous materials
requiring placards in any train.

## § 213.53–213.63 Gage, alinement, and surface

Standard gage is 4 feet 8 1/2 inches (56.5 inches) measured between rail heads
at right angles 5/8 inch below the top of the rail head. Gage limits by class:
Class 1 track must be between 4'8" (56 in) and 4'10" (58 in); tighter limits
apply as class increases (e.g., Class 4 and 5 maximum gage 57 1/2 in).
Alinement and surface deviation limits are tabulated per class in §§ 213.55
and 213.63.

## § 213.113–213.119 Rail defects and joints

When an owner learns that a rail contains a listed defect (e.g., transverse
fissure, detail fracture, broken base), remedial action from the table in
§ 213.113 must be taken — ranging from immediate repair to operating at
reduced speed with periodic inspection until repair. Continuous welded rail
(CWR) must be installed and maintained under a written CWR plan (§ 213.119)
addressing rail neutral temperature, de-stressing, and inspection of joints.

## § 213.233–213.241 Track inspection

Track must be visually inspected on a defined frequency by a qualified
inspector: Class 1, 2, and 3 track carrying passenger trains or over 10
million gross tons annually — twice weekly with at least a one calendar day
interval; other Class 1–3 track — weekly (or before use if used less than
once a week); Class 4 and 5 — twice weekly. Special inspections are required
after fires, floods, severe storms, or other occurrences that might damage
track. Inspection records must identify the inspector, date, track inspected,
and any deviations found, and be kept at least one year (two years for
internal rail inspection records).

## § 213.237 Inspection of rail (internal defects)

Rail on Class 4 and 5 track, and Class 3 track carrying passenger trains or
more than 10 million gross tons of annual traffic, must be periodically
inspected for internal defects (ultrasonic or equivalent), with frequency
driven by tonnage and defect history.
