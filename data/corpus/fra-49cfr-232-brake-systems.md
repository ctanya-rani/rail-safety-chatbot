## § 232.103 General train brake requirements

A train's air brake system must be able to stop the train with a service
brake application within the signal spacing or other operating limits. At
least 85 percent of the cars in a train must have operative brakes for the
train to depart a Class I brake test location, and 100 percent of brakes
must be operative when departing an initial terminal. Unattended equipment
must be secured with a sufficient number of hand brakes, verified by
demonstrating the securement holds (push-pull test), per the railroad's
securement rules.

## § 232.205 Class I brake test — initial terminal inspection

A Class I brake test by a qualified person must be performed: at a train's
initial terminal, when a train's consist is changed beyond adding/removing a
solid block of cars, when a train is off air for more than the permitted
period (generally 24 hours under specified conditions, otherwise 4 hours),
and at 1,000-mile intervals (extended-haul trains under § 232.213 may run up
to 1,500 miles between tests subject to conditions). The test verifies brake
pipe integrity and leakage, application and release on each car, piston
travel within limits, and brake rigging condition.

## § 232.207–232.211 Class IA, Class II, and Class III brake tests

A Class IA brake test is performed at 1,000-mile intervals where a Class I
test is not required. A Class II brake test applies where cars are added
that previously received a Class I test and remained charged. A Class III
brake test (application and release on the rear car, brake pipe continuity)
is required when a locomotive or caboose is changed, or after any separation
of the brake pipe, before proceeding.

## § 232.303–232.309 Single car air brake tests

Each freight car must receive a single car air brake test when placed in
service after repair for a brake defect, and periodically per AAR standard
S-486 as incorporated. Cars found with defective brakes en route may be
moved for repair under designated-repair-point rules with defect tags.

## § 232.409 End-of-train devices

Two-way end-of-train (EOT) telemetry devices are required on trains
operating over 30 mph or on heavy grades, enabling emergency brake
application from the rear of the train. The rear unit's brake pipe pressure
display must be visible to the crew, and devices must be tested and
calibrated per the manufacturer's schedule (at least annually).
