## Article 2 Scope — when the CSM-RA applies

The CSM on risk evaluation and assessment applies when any change to the
railway system (technical, operational, or organisational — where the
organisational change could impact operating or maintenance processes) is
judged **significant**. The proposer decides significance using the
Article 4 criteria; if the change is not significant, it is managed under
the proposer's own SMS with the decision documented.

## Article 4 Significance criteria

Significance is judged by expert assessment against six criteria:
1. failure consequence — credible worst-case scenario in the event of
   failure of the system under assessment;
2. novelty used in implementing the change (novel to the sector or to the
   organisation);
3. complexity of the change;
4. monitoring — inability to monitor the implemented change throughout the
   system life cycle and intervene appropriately;
5. reversibility — inability to revert to the system as it was before;
6. additionality — assessment of the change in view of other recent
   safety-related modifications that were not judged significant.

## Article 5 and Annex I — Risk management process

For significant changes, the proposer must apply the risk management process
in Annex I: system definition, hazard identification and classification, and
risk analysis using one or more of the three **risk acceptance principles**:

- application of **codes of practice**;
- comparison with **similar reference systems**;
- **explicit risk estimation** (quantitative or qualitative), where broadly
  acceptable risk may be shown, and for technical systems where a functional
  failure has a credible direct potential for catastrophic consequences,
  a quantitative requirement of no more than 10⁻⁹ dangerous failures per
  operating hour applies (harmonised design target).

All hazards and safety requirements must be tracked in a **hazard record**
kept up to date throughout the system life cycle.

## Article 6 Independent assessment (Assessment Body)

An independent assessment body (AsBo) must assess the correct application of
the risk management process and its results before the change is accepted.
The AsBo may be an accredited/recognised body or the NSA acting as such, and
must meet the independence and competence criteria of Annex II. The AsBo
delivers a safety assessment report on which the proposer relies for its
declaration.

## Article 15–16 Safety assessment report and mutual recognition

The proposer issues a written declaration that all identified hazards and
associated risks are controlled to an acceptable level. Safety assessment
reports of AsBos are mutually recognised across Member States for equivalent
conditions of use.
