## § 217.7–217.9 Operating rules; program of operational tests

Each railroad (except those with fewer than 400,000 total employee work
hours annually, which have reduced filing duties) must keep a current copy of
its code of operating rules, timetables, and timetable special instructions
at its system headquarters and make them available to FRA. Each railroad must
conduct a program of operational tests and inspections (efficiency testing)
to determine the extent of compliance with its operating rules, maintain
written records of the program and of each test performed, and periodically
review test data and adjust the program accordingly.

## § 217.11 Program of instruction on operating rules

Each railroad must periodically instruct each employee whose activities are
governed by the operating rules on the meaning and application of those
rules, and keep a written program of instruction on file.

## § 218.22–218.30 Blue signal protection of workers

Workers inspecting, testing, repairing, or servicing rolling equipment must
be protected by blue signals. A blue signal displayed at one or both ends of
rolling equipment means the equipment may not be coupled to, moved, or have
its brakes released, and other equipment may not pass the signal. Blue
signals may be removed only by the same craft or group that placed them.
Specific provisions cover workers on main track, in yards, and utility
employees attached to a train crew.

## § 218.35–218.37 Protection of occupied camp cars and flagging

Occupied camp cars require protections similar to blue signal protection.
Where required by railroad rule, flag protection must be provided against
following trains on the same track.

## Subpart F — Handling equipment, switches, and fixed derails

Railroads must adopt and comply with operating rules covering: leaving
rolling equipment in the clear (§ 218.93 clearance point), securement of
unattended equipment with hand brakes (§ 218.99 shoving movements are
governed by § 218.99 point protection — a crew member must visually
determine the track is clear), and the position of switches and derails.
Employees operating hand-operated main track switches in non-signaled
territory must follow job briefing and switch position awareness rules
(§ 218.105–218.109), including conducting a job briefing before work and
reporting the switch position to the dispatcher.
