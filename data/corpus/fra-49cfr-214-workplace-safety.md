## Subpart B — Bridge worker safety

Railroad employees working on bridges more than 12 feet above the ground or
water surface must be protected by a fall protection system (safety net,
harness system, or scaffolding with railings). Personal protective equipment
(hard hats, foot protection, eye protection) is required as appropriate to
the hazard.

## § 214.303–214.309 On-track safety programs

Each railroad must adopt and comply with a written on-track safety program
that provides roadway workers protection from trains and on-track equipment.
The program must be retained at the railroad's system headquarters and made
available to FRA on request. Each roadway worker has the absolute right to
challenge in good faith whether the on-track safety being provided complies
with the program, and to refuse an assignment until the challenge is
resolved (good-faith challenge, § 214.311).

## § 214.313–214.319 Individual responsibility and working limits

No roadway worker may foul a track (be within four feet of the field side of
the near rail) without an on-track safety procedure in place. Working limits
must be under the control of a single roadway worker in charge. Forms of
working limits on controlled track include exclusive track occupancy,
foul time, and train coordination; on non-controlled track, inaccessibility
(lined and locked switches, derails).

## § 214.329–214.337 Train approach warning and lone workers

Roadway workers may be protected by watchmen/lookouts providing train
approach warning. Warning must be given in time for workers to move to a
predetermined place of safety at least 15 seconds before a train moving at
maximum authorized speed could reach the work location. A lone worker
performing routine inspection or minor correction may use individual train
detection only where sight distance is adequate, and must complete a written
statement of on-track safety before fouling the track.

## § 214.343–214.353 Training and qualification

Roadway workers, watchmen/lookouts, flagmen, and roadway workers in charge
must be trained and qualified initially and at least annually on the on-track
safety rules applicable to their duties, with records of qualification
maintained.

## Subpart D — Roadway maintenance machines and hi-rail vehicles

Roadway maintenance machines must have operable brakes, horns, and — for
new machines — rollover protection and operator protection against falling
objects. Hi-rail vehicles must be inspected for on-track safety compliance
at least annually and defects recorded and repaired.
